## 4.  The turf question — why the 10/20 memorandum does not close L-2

Adrienne Vogel's memorandum of 10/20/2026 concludes that the nonfunctional turf prohibition does not apply to Phase 3 because the property is zoned R-2 detached and is mapped single-family residential, and asks us to adopt that position in the response matrix and record L-2 as closed. We are not able to do that, and we would be doing Callaway no service by sealing a landscape sheet that says so.

The administering agency's own compliance bulletin — SNWA WRC-2024-07, which supersedes WRC-2022-04 and carries the 12/31/2026 compliance deadline — addresses the point directly. It applies to "all properties within the SNWA service area other than individually-owned single-family residential lots," and then closes the door on the project-level reading:

"The single-family residential carve-out is lot-based, not project-based."

"HOA-owned or HOA-managed common-area parcels of any description, including but not limited to park parcels, tot lots, greenbelts, drainage tracts, entry features, monument islands, and landscape maintenance strips" remain "fully subject to the prohibition even where they lie within a subdivision the zoning of which is single-family residential."

"the presence of a single-family residential zoning designation on the underlying parcel does not confer the carve-out on common-area turf within that parcel; the operative distinction is the lot line, not the zoning district."

The Phase 3 park is Common-Area Parcel "D" on the final map and on the civil plans (Sheet C-2), and it is called Parcel A on the Phase 3 landscape sheets. Those are two labels for the same 1.85-acre parcel. It is not the Parcel A of the recorded declaration: the CC&Rs recorded 12/23/2024 (Instrument No. 20241223-0004422, Book 175, Page 61) define the "Common-Area Park" as Parcel A on the first final map, which is the Phases 1 and 2 park. Phase 3 common area becomes subject to the declaration automatically on recordation of the Phase 3 map (§3.2(c)), and the declarant may annex Phase 3 by supplemental declaration (§3.3); Condition 16 separately requires CC&Rs establishing the association's common-area maintenance obligations, with a common-area maintenance exhibit, to be recorded concurrently with the Phase 3 map. Association ownership and maintenance of Parcel D is therefore an obligation to be perfected on recordation, not a completed conveyance today — but under the bulletin the parcel is squarely inside the prohibition either way, because the carve-out turns on the lot line and no part of Parcel D lies inside a single-family lot.

On the functional categories, our own tabulation decides the question against us. It records 0 square feet of Phase 3 turf "within a designated play area with playground amenities." The playground equipment — slides, swings and climbing structures — was value-engineered out in July 2026 — our coordination email of 28 July 2026 records the cut — and Sheet L-3 now describes the park as "a passive-use turf lawn." Bulletin §3.1 requires amenities that are "installed and operable" and states that "a designated play area lacking installed playground amenities does not qualify as playground turf, regardless of the sign or plan label attached to the area." §4 adds that such an area "cannot be waived under Section 3.1 by promising future amenities," and that "a lawn intended for passive use, decorative purpose, aesthetic frontage, or general open space cannot be waived under Section 3.2 by asserting occasional recreational use."

The contrast with Phase 1 and 2 is the tell. The approved 2022 set carried 4,200 square feet of turf "within a clearly designated play area" with slides, swings and climbing structures installed, and its compliance note said so. Phase 3 carries 18,400 square feet — more than four times as much — with none of the qualifying elements. The 18,400 square feet of cool-season sod at Parcel D (Parcel A on the landscape sheets) constitutes nonfunctional turf, and its conversion deadline is 12/31/2026.

There is a further point that forecloses the "we will deal with it when we build" fallback. The bulletin states that on and after 12/31/2026 "the presence of nonfunctional turf on a property subject to the prohibition constitutes a continuing violation." Installing new nonfunctional turf after the deadline is not a lawful alternative to converting existing turf; the prohibition is on the turf, not on the date it was planted. Deferring the decision to construction does not preserve the option.

Calculations, so that the reviewer can follow them line by line:

- Spray turf: 18,400 sf × 5.5 ft/yr = 101,200 cu ft ÷ 43,560 = 2.323 ac-ft.
- Drip planting, as issued: 22,200 sf × 1.0 ft/yr = 22,200 cu ft ÷ 43,560 = 0.510 ac-ft; with the 18,400 sf converted: 40,600 cu ft ÷ 43,560 = 0.932 ac-ft.

- Totals: 2.323 + 0.510 + 0.454 = 3.287; 2.323 + 0.510 + 1.375 = 4.208 (+0.921); 0.932 + 1.375 = 2.307 (−0.980).
Rounding: the computed totals are 3.287 / 4.208 / 2.307.
